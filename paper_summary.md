The paper How to Read a Paper by S. Keshav explains a three-pass approach for reading research papers without spending hundreds of hours every year.The main idea is to read a paper in three passes instead of trying to understand everything at once.The first pass focuses on getting the overall picture by looking at the title, abstract, introduction, headings, conclusion, and references.The goal is to understand what the paper is about, what problem it is trying to solve, and what the main contribution is.The second pass focuses on diving deeper into the content,particularly the figures, diagrams, and important references, while still avoiding unnecessary details.The third pass focuses on we try to understand the paper deeply, including how the authors reached their results, what assumptions they made, and whether there are any weaknesses in their approach.

What I found most useful was the idea that reading a research paper is not the same as reading a textbook. Previously, I would tend to read from the first page to the last reading everything with almost the same amount of focus but now I learnt a better approach.This paper showed me that it is better to first understand the big picture and then gradually go deeper.The three pass method made me realise that purpose should be understood before diving into the technical details instantly. The thing that I felt the most interesting was questioning the assumptions rather than blindly believing them. This alerts the reader from merely reading to actually understanding.

Hence the next time I read a research paper, I want to use this three-pass approach. I will ask myself "How will I solve the problem that is being addressed ?" and then the read the research paper to agree or disagree with the writer's point of view.This approach will help me becpme a curious learner and a critical thinker.
